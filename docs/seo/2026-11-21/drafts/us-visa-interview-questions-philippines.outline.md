## Target reader need

A Filipino applicant (most often a first-time tourist or family visitor, sometimes a J-1 exchange visitor or F-1 student) with an interview at the U.S. Embassy in Manila wants to know the common questions, how to explain work, income, family and trip plans clearly, what to bring, what language the interview can be in, and how to prepare without fixers or scripts.

## Outline (H2/H3)

1. **Intro:** what this guide covers (visitor-led, with student and exchange sections) and what it does not (appointments, fees, official checklists).
2. **H2: Three things to confirm before you practise**
   - Your visa category (B1/B2, F-1, J-1, H-1B/H-4) and which guide fits.
   - Where you apply: NIV applicants should interview in their country of residence or nationality; fees are non-refundable and non-transferable (ph.usembassy.gov notice, read 2026-11-21).
   - Your own appointment instructions are the final word on documents and entry rules.
3. **H2: What a visitor (B1/B2) interview in Manila is checking** (purpose, intent to return, ability to pay; 214(b) in plain words)
4. **H2: Common US tourist visa questions for Filipino applicants**
   - H3: Purpose and plans (visiting relatives in the U.S., tourism, events)
   - H3: Work, business and income (employee, BPO or night-shift worker, small-business owner, freelancer, retiree: describe your own role and income truthfully)
   - H3: Family at home and who you are visiting in the U.S.
   - H3: Who pays (your own funds vs a relative's support; amounts in PHP and USD)
   - H3: Travel history and previous refusals
5. **H2: Example answers: vague versus specific** (fictional, labelled)
6. **H2: If you work abroad or live outside the Philippines** (overseas workers): apply where you reside or in your country of nationality; applying elsewhere can make it harder to qualify and means longer waits. Link the any-country post.
7. **H2: Students and exchange visitors (F-1 and J-1)**
   - F-1: school choice, program, funding, plans; Form I-20; SEVIS I-901 fee ($350 for F-1 as of 2026-11-21).
   - J-1: program, sponsor, DS-2019, funding, two-year home-residency awareness; I-901 $220 (or $35 for au pair, camp counselor and summer work travel; $0 for government-sponsored programs).
   - Social media "public" instruction for F, M and J applicants (embassy notice).
   - Link the F1 and J1 guides.
8. **H2: H-1B and H-4 applicants** (two sentences each, link the H1B and H4 guides; social media instruction applies to both)
9. **H2: What to bring and what to wear**
   - Core items: passport valid six months beyond the stay unless exempt, DS-160 confirmation page, fee receipt if required, appointment confirmation; then "follow the list in your appointment instructions".
   - Electronic devices and bag rules: the embassy's immigrant-visa instructions say phones and electronic devices cannot be brought inside; for nonimmigrant appointments, follow your own instructions (do not generalize).
   - Attire: no official dress code was found (2026-11-21). Practical advice only: neat, comfortable clothes you would wear to a formal appointment.
10. **H2: Interview language: English or Filipino?**

    - F-1 and J-1 applicants in English-taught programs should expect to use English.

## Original examples and checklist ideas

- **Four fictional applicant cards** (labelled fictional): a Cebu-based nurse visiting a sister in California; a Quezon City BPO team lead attending a cousin's wedding; a retired couple from Iloilo visiting a son who is a U.S. citizen; a J-1 hospitality intern with a DS-2019. Each card: purpose answer, money answer, home-ties answer, likely follow-up.
- **"Work and income in one breath" exercise:** job title, employer, how long, schedule, monthly income in PHP, who covers the trip. Practise saying it in under 20 seconds.
- **Peso-to-dollar funding note:** a small table converting the trip budget, with the date of the exchange rate used, so the applicant can answer consistently.
- **Overseas-worker decision box:** "I live and work in (country) → where should I apply?" with a pointer to the residence guidance; no promises about outcomes.
- **Day-before checklist:** appointment confirmation, DS-160 confirmation page, passport validity check, I-20 or DS-2019 plus I-901 receipt for students and exchange visitors, and the embassy's own instructions printed or saved.

## FAQ (frontmatter; 4-5 questions)

1. *Can I do my U.S. visa interview in Filipino?* The embassy's immigrant-visa instructions treat English or Filipino as interview languages (interpreter only if you speak neither well enough). For a nonimmigrant appointment, follow your own instructions. Students should expect English.
2. *What should I wear to the U.S. Embassy in Manila?* No official dress code was found; wear neat, comfortable clothes. What matters is clear, truthful answers.
3. *Do I need a sponsor or an invitation letter?* An invitation letter is not required to apply for a visitor visa (State Department visitor guidance; recheck). If a relative pays, explain exactly what they pay for.
4. *How long is the interview?* There is no official duration. Prepare short, direct answers and expect follow-ups.
5. *Does the Philippines refusal rate mean I will be refused?* No. The FY2025 adjusted B refusal rate (30.76%) is a national statistic; each case is decided on its own facts.
