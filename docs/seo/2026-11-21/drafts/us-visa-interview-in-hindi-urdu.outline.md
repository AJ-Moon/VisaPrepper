## Target reader need

An applicant in India or Pakistan (or a family member helping them) who is more comfortable in Hindi or Urdu wants to know whether the real consular interview can be held in that language, what happens if their English is limited, and how to practise common questions in Hindi or Urdu without their facts drifting from the English DS-160.

## Outline (H2/H3)

1. **Intro:** the short answer depends on the embassy, the visa category and the applicant; this post shows what official instructions say and how to practise in the language you think in.
2. **H2: The short answer**
   - No single worldwide rule was found. Embassies publish their own language and interpreter instructions; the officer runs the interview.
3. **H2: What official instructions say about Hindi and Urdu**
   - New Delhi immigrant-visa instructions: bring one interpreter only if you do not speak English, Hindi, Punjabi, Malayalam, Tibetan or Tamil well enough (archived copy, medium).
   - Islamabad immigrant-visa instructions: bring one interpreter only if you do not speak English or Urdu well enough (archived copy, medium).
   - These are immigrant-visa (green-card) instructions. The India and Pakistan embassy visa pages checked on 2026-11-21 did not state a language for nonimmigrant (visitor, student, work) interviews: say "unknown, check your appointment instructions".
   - Example of a post that states it for visitors: the U.S. Embassy in Dhaka says officers interview in Bangla or English and "English competency is not a requirement" (read directly 2026-11-21). Use only as an illustration that posts set their own rules.
4. **H2: When English matters more**
   - The DS-160 is completed in English, except your name in your native alphabet (medium).
   - Students: one embassy tells F-1 students whose I-20 shows English proficiency to be ready to demonstrate it (vn.usembassy.gov, read 2026-11-21). An Indian or Pakistani student in an English-taught program should expect to answer in English.
   - H-1B: job titles, duties and employer details are usually in English on your petition; practise saying them both ways.
5. **H2: Common questions with Hindi and Urdu practice prompts**
   - One table per section: English question | Hindi prompt (Devanagari) | Urdu prompt (Urdu script) | what the question checks.
   - H3: Visitor (B1/B2) questions (purpose, stay length, who pays, work at home, travel history)
   - H3: Student (F-1) questions (why this university, funding, plans after study)
   - H3: Work and dependent (H-1B, H-4) questions (role, employer, worksite; marriage and spouse's job)
   - Prompts must be written or reviewed by a native Hindi speaker and a native Urdu speaker before publishing. **No machine translation without review, and no full translated answers.**
6. **H2: Change the language, not the facts**
   - Names, dates, amounts and employer names must match the English DS-160 and documents; practise numbers in both languages.
   - Transliteration of names: use the spelling in your passport.
7. **H2: If you need an interpreter** (follow your post's interpreter rule; an interpreter translates, they do not answer for you)
8. **H2: A 15-minute bilingual practice routine** (answer in Hindi or Urdu, then in English; record; compare with the DS-160; repeat the weakest answer)
9. **H2: Practise your U.S. interview in Hindi, Urdu or English** (CTA)
10. **FAQ** (frontmatter `faq`)

## Original examples and checklist ideas

- **Bilingual fact card:** the same 6 facts (purpose, dates, who pays, job, family at home, previous travel) written once in English and once in Hindi or Urdu, so the applicant sees that only the language changes.
- **Numbers drill:** amounts in INR or PKR and in USD, dates and durations said aloud in both languages (common source of accidental inconsistency).
- **"Translator trap" examples** (fictional): a family member answering for the applicant; a translated job title that sounds like a different job; an amount converted from lakh/crore incorrectly. Each with the correct approach.
- **Language-switch check:** if the officer switches to English mid-interview, answer the question asked; practise short English versions of every answer.
- **Practice table design note:** keep prompts short (one line each); this is an MDX table using the existing table component, so no design change.

## FAQ (frontmatter; 4 questions)

1. *Can I give my U.S. visa interview in Hindi?* Embassy instructions for immigrant-visa interviews in New Delhi list Hindi among the languages that need no interpreter. For visitor, student and work visas, no official language statement was found on 2026-11-21: check your appointment instructions.
2. *Is Urdu allowed at the U.S. Embassy in Islamabad?* The Islamabad immigrant-visa instructions say you need an interpreter only if you do not speak English or Urdu well enough. Confirm the instructions for your own appointment.
3. *Can I fill in the DS-160 in Hindi or Urdu?* Answers are entered in English, except your full name in your native alphabet (State Department DS-160 FAQ; recheck).
4. *Does practising in Hindi or Urdu help if my interview is in English?* It helps you organize true facts; finish each practice by giving the same answer in short English.

Avoid any FAQ that claims the language you choose affects (or does not affect) the decision; no official statement on that was found.
