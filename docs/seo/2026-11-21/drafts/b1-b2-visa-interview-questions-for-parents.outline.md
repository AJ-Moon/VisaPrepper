## Target reader need

A parent, often retired, who has been invited by an adult son or daughter living in the U.S. (as a student, H-1B worker, green-card holder or citizen) wants to know what the consular officer will ask, how to explain the visit honestly in their own words, who pays for what, how long they plan to stay and what their child can provide. The adult child frequently does the research and helps the parent practise.

## Outline (H2/H3)

1. **Intro:** a visit to see a son or daughter is a normal B-2 purpose; the officer still needs to understand the trip, the money and the return. The parent answers, in their own words.
2. **H2: What the officer is checking when a parent visits**
   - B-2 "pleasure" includes visits with friends or relatives (22 CFR 41.31(b)(2)(i); 9 FAM 402.2-4(B)(1)).
   - Three conditions: intent to leave at the end of the stay, permission to return home, adequate financial arrangements (22 CFR 41.31(a)).
   - 214(b) presumption and burden (9 FAM 302.1-2), stated in plain words.
3. **H2: Who answers, and in which language**
   - The parent answers; the child usually is not in the interview (check the appointment instructions).
   - Practise in the language the parent will actually use; link the Hindi/Urdu post once published, `/languages` now.
4. **H2: Questions about the purpose of your visit**
   - H3: "Why are you visiting the United States?" (graduation, a new grandchild, a family visit, sightseeing)
   - H3: "Who will you stay with, and what does your son or daughter do there?" (status, employer or university, city: know the basics without guessing)
   - H3: "How long will you stay, and when will you return?" (dates; the visa's validity is not the length of stay)
5. **H2: Questions about money: who pays for what**
   - Split flights, accommodation, daily costs and any medical costs between the parent's own funds and the child's support.
   - An invitation letter is not required to apply and does not decide the case (visitor page, medium).
6. **H2: Questions about home: what brings you back**
   - Pension or business, property, a spouse or other children at home, health care, community roles.
   - A retired parent describes their real routine; do not invent employment (consistent with the B1/B2 guide).
7. **H2: Travel history and previous refusals** (be precise about earlier U.S. or other visas; link the 214(b) guide)
8. **H2: When both parents apply together**
   - Each person files their own DS-160; shared plans should match because they are the same plans.
9. **H2: Older parents and the in-person interview**
   - Since Oct 1, 2025, applicants over 79 generally need an in-person interview; the main waiver left is renewing a full-validity B1/B2 visa within 12 months of expiry, if all conditions are met (dated, medium).
   - Some embassies allow one helper for elderly applicants; check your post's instructions (do not generalize one post's rule).
10. **H2: If your nationality is on the visa-bond list** (short, dated): nationals of listed countries (for example Bangladesh and Nepal, listed from Jan 21, 2026 on the archived State list) may be asked to post a bond after the officer finds them otherwise eligible; amount set at the interview. Readers from other countries can skip this.
11. **H2: Medical treatment or a birth in the family**
    - If medical treatment is a purpose, the regulation asks for a legitimate reason, a U.S. provider who agreed to treat, estimated duration and costs, and lawful means to pay (22 CFR 41.31(b)(2)(ii)).
    - Visiting to help after a grandchild's birth: say so plainly. The birth-tourism rule is about travel mainly to give birth in the U.S. (22 CFR 41.31; 9 FAM 402.2-4(B)(8)); do not coach anyone on avoiding questions.
12. **H2: Documents to review with your child before the interview**
    - Core items: passport valid six months beyond the stay unless exempt, DS-160 confirmation page, fee receipt if required.
    - Optional records a parent may keep ready: the child's status document and U.S. address, a short note of who pays what, the parent's own income or pension and property records. Show them only if asked.
    - Link the documents checklist and `/document-checks`.
13. **H2: A 20-minute practice session for parents** (the child asks, the parent answers in their language, then they check every answer against the DS-160)
14. **H2: Practise the interview with your own details** (CTA)
15. **FAQ** (frontmatter `faq`)

## Original examples and checklist ideas

- **Three fictional parent profiles** (labelled fictional): a retired teacher from Pune visiting a daughter on H-1B in Texas for a grandchild's birth; a shop-owning couple from Lahore attending a son's graduation; a widowed mother from Manila visiting a son who is a U.S. citizen. For each: purpose answer, money split, home ties, the follow-up the officer is likely to ask, and the mistake to avoid.
- **"Who pays for what" table:** rows for flights, insurance, lodging, daily costs, medical; columns for parent, child, other; amounts and currency. The parent should be able to explain each row.
- **"What your child can prepare" checklist:** status document copy, address, work or study schedule during the visit, a short note in plain words. Mark clearly that none of these is an official requirement and none guarantees anything.
- **Dates sanity check:** departure, return booking intent (not purchased), the child's academic or work calendar, and the parent's commitments at home.
- **Pitfall list:** saying "my son will decide when I return", listing a job the parent does not hold, memorizing the child's script, different trip lengths given by two parents.

## FAQ (frontmatter; 4 questions)

1. *Does my son or daughter need to send an invitation letter?* Not required to apply and not a deciding factor, per the State Department's visitor guidance (medium; recheck). Bring one only if it explains real facts.
2. *Can my son or daughter come to the interview with me?* Usually only applicants enter; some posts allow one helper for elderly applicants. Follow your appointment instructions.
3. *My parents are over 80. Do they still need an interview?* Since Oct 1, 2025, applicants over 79 generally interview in person unless they qualify for a renewal waiver (medium; recheck the live notice).
4. *Can practising guarantee a visa for my parents?* No. Practice helps them explain true facts clearly; the consular officer decides.
