## Target reader need

The foreign spouse of a U.S. citizen (IR1/CR1), or of a green-card holder (F2A, mentioned briefly), whose Form I-130 was approved and whose case is moving through the National Visa Center to an embassy interview, wants to know what the consular officer will ask about the relationship, the petitioner, finances, prior marriages and the DS-260, which documents to organize, and how to prepare honestly without memorizing a script with their spouse.

## Outline (H2/H3)

1. **Intro:** who this is for; what the officer decides; preparation is about explaining a real shared history, not passing a quiz.
2. **H2: Who this guide is for (and who it is not for)**
   - IR1 and CR1 (spouse of a U.S. citizen), F2A briefly (spouse of a permanent resident; a visa number must be available).
   - Not USCIS interviews inside the U.S.; not K-1 fiancé(e) visas.
3. **H2: Where the interview fits in the process**
   - Petitioner files Form I-130; after approval the case goes to consular processing; the embassy or consulate sends notification and processing information (USCIS spouse page).
   - Then: DS-260, civil documents, affidavit of support, panel-physician medical exam, interview, visa, USCIS Immigrant Fee before travel.
4. **H2: What the consular officer is deciding**
   - Every immigrant-visa applicant appears in person (a child under 14 can be excused at the officer's discretion); the officer determines classification and eligibility and may require answers to any material question (22 CFR 42.62).
   - You confirm and sign your DS-260 at the interview; if something must change, the change is made before you swear to it (22 CFR 42.67(a)(3)).
5. **H2: Relationship history questions**
   - How and when you met; first meeting in person; how the relationship developed; engagement and wedding; who attended; time spent together; languages you speak together.
6. **H2: Married life and future plans**
   - Where and how you lived together or visited; how you stay in touch; where you will live in the U.S.; work or study plans.
7. **H2: Questions about your petitioning spouse**
   - Their job, employer, city and address; how they became a citizen or permanent resident; prior marriages and children. Know the basics; say "I don't know" rather than guess.
8. **H2: Prior marriages, children and family background**
   - Proof that earlier marriages ended (divorce, annulment, death certificate); children from either side.
9. **H2: The affidavit of support and money questions**
   - Form I-864 is a legally binding contract with the U.S. government; joint sponsors; tax transcripts. Only the 08/24/26 edition is accepted from Oct 1, 2026 (USCIS I-864 page). Do not give income thresholds unless rechecked on the day of writing.
10. **H2: DS-260 consistency: check before you swear to it**
    - Names and aliases, addresses, employment, travel, prior U.S. visas and refusals, prior stays. Link the DS-160 post for the review method (the forms differ; the habit is the same).
11. **H2: Documents to organize**
    - Generic list grounded in 22 CFR 42.65/42.66 (police certificates, birth record, relationship records, medical exam) plus one embassy example (Manila's list: appointment letter, passport valid six months beyond entry, DS-260 confirmation page, birth certificate, police certificate, I-864 with the sponsor's IRS transcript, proof of the petitioner's status, evidence of the relationship such as photographs, letters and chat logs, marriage certificate, proof prior marriages ended).
    - Bring originals: the Manila embassy says that since May 19, 2025, immigrant-visa applicants without all required originals will not be interviewed (one post's rule; check yours).
12. **H2: IR1 or CR1: what changes after the visa**
    - If married less than two years when permanent residence is granted, residence is conditional (two-year card); remove conditions with Form I-751 in the 90 days before the card expires. Know this; it is not an interview topic.
13. **H2: Practise together without writing a script**
    - Each spouse answers alone from real memories; compare facts afterwards, never wording. Matches the FAQ already on `/visa-types/family-immigrant`.
14. **H2: Rules that depend on nationality (check before the interview)**
    - Proclamation 10998 (from Jan 1, 2026) suspends immigrant visas for nationals of listed countries; its categorical exceptions do not include spouses of U.S. citizens (the earlier Proclamation 10949 exceptions were superseded for those countries). State the fact, cite it and tell readers from listed countries to check current State Department guidance. No legal conclusions.
    - The Jan 2026 immigrant-visa pause for 75 nationalities ended Aug 21, 2026 under a court order (medium); mention only if still relevant on publish day.
15. **H2: Practise your spouse interview with your own details** (CTA)
16. **FAQ** (frontmatter `faq`)

## Original examples and checklist ideas

- **Relationship timeline worksheet:** first contact, first meeting, key visits, engagement, wedding, periods apart, current living arrangement. Each spouse fills it alone, then they compare dates, not phrasing.
- **"Know your petitioner" card:** employer, job title, city, how they got status, prior marriages, children. Mark "unsure" items to confirm, not to memorize.
- **Three fictional case sketches** (labelled fictional): a couple who met online and married after two in-person visits (CR1); a couple married four years with a child (IR1, IR2 child); a couple with a prior divorce on one side. Each with the follow-up an officer might ask and the document that supports it.
- **DS-260 "swear-ready" checklist:** names and aliases, every address and job the form asks for, employment history, prior U.S. travel and refusals, police certificates for each country lived in six months or more (per 42.65 definitions).
- **Original-documents box:** originals plus translations; check your post's checklist; some posts refuse to interview without all originals.

## FAQ (frontmatter; 5 questions)

1. *Does my U.S. spouse need to attend the interview?* Several embassies say petitioners are not required to attend (for example Manila and Islamabad in their immigrant-visa instructions). Follow your own appointment letter.
2. *What relationship evidence do embassies ask for?* It varies by post. Manila's checklist, for example, lists photographs, letters and chat logs. Bring what is true and organized; quantity is not the goal.
3. *Is this the same as a USCIS marriage interview?* No. This guide covers the consular interview abroad for an immigrant visa. USCIS adjustment-of-status interviews happen inside the U.S.
4. *What if my DS-260 has a mistake?* Tell the officer. You confirm the DS-260 at the interview, and the regulation provides for required changes before you swear to it.
5. *Can practice guarantee my spouse visa?* No. Practice helps you explain your real history clearly; the consular officer decides.
