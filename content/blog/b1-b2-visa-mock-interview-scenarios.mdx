Reading visitor-visa questions and answering them out loud are different tasks. A first answer may sound reasonable while leaving the dates, budget or purpose of the trip unclear. This mock-interview workshop helps you explore those gaps with follow-ups.

Use our [B1/B2 interview question guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) for the topic reference. This page is a rehearsal routine: choose a relevant scenario, gather your real facts and practice responding to a changing question.

## First check that the scenario fits your real trip

The Department of State's [visitor visa guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) distinguishes temporary business activities from tourism and visits. A visitor category is not permission to take U.S. employment. If the planned activity does not fit, do not rename it to make a practice answer sound acceptable; verify the appropriate category.

All scenarios below are fictional exercises. Do not adopt a family arrangement, job, invitation or itinerary simply because it appears in a practice question.

## Build a fact sheet before the mock interview

Make six short notes: purpose, intended dates, accommodation, funding, current commitments and relevant travel history. Mark tentative plans as tentative. Compare these with your application details and any records you rely on.

If a form says you will fund the trip but a relative is paying some costs, understand the actual split. If the facts have changed since submission, follow the official correction instructions rather than practicing an answer you know is wrong. Our [DS-160 review guide](/blog/ds-160-interview-consistency) explains what to check.

## Scenario 1: a tourism visit

**Starting question:** What do you plan to do during your visit?

Give a brief answer using your actual intended activities and timing. Your practice partner then chooses a question that follows from what you said:

- Which places do you intend to visit, and how does the travel fit your available time?
- Where do you plan to stay? What is confirmed and what is still tentative?
- How did you estimate the cost of flights, accommodation and day-to-day expenses?
- Who pays for each part of the trip?
- How does the intended visit fit your work, study or other responsibilities afterward?

**Review task:** Did you explain a coherent trip or list attractions without connecting them to time and cost? Do not buy tickets merely to make a rehearsal more concrete. The official visitor guidance advises against final travel plans or ticket purchases before a visa is issued.

## Scenario 2: visiting family or friends

**Starting question:** Who will you visit, and how long do you intend to stay?

Use your real relationship and plans. Follow with questions such as:

- Where will you stay, and what arrangements have you actually discussed?
- Who will cover travel and living expenses?
- Why have you chosen that length of visit?
- What are your plans after the visit?
- Have you visited before, and what happened on that trip?

**Review task:** Distinguish an invitation, a place to stay and funding. They are separate facts. Do not assume that a relative's willingness to host establishes every other part of your case. Each applicant should understand their own plans instead of relying on another family member's memorized response.

## Scenario 3: a temporary business trip

**Starting question:** What business activity takes you to the United States?

Practice naming the actual meeting, conference or other permitted purpose. Then explore:

- What is your current role, and how does the activity relate to it?
- Who will you meet and what will you discuss?
- Why do you need to attend in person?
- Which organization covers flights, accommodation or other expenses?
- What will you do when the activity finishes?

**Review task:** Can you explain the activity rather than just say “business”? If an invitation or employer note exists, understand it without reciting it. Do not misdescribe a job or productive work as a visitor meeting. Use official guidance or qualified advice if the category is unclear.

## Practice the follow-up hidden inside a general answer

| General statement | Follow-up to try | What to resolve beforehand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I have enough savings.” | Which expenses are you covering and how did you budget for them? | Actual funds, costs, period and currency |
| “I will stay with family.” | Where, for how long, and what has been agreed? | Real hosting arrangements and tentative details |
| “My company is sending me.” | For which activity, in what role, and who pays? | Employer, purpose and cost allocation |
| “I will return to my work.” | What are your genuine plans after the trip? | Actual commitments; no invented leave letters or offers |

A missing detail is not proof that you lack money or intend to overstay. It is a reason to understand and explain your circumstances more clearly. Better wording does not replace a fact you have not checked.

## Run a short spoken session

1. Choose the scenario closest to your real purpose. Let a partner reorder the questions, or select prompts randomly for solo practice.
2. Answer the first question without reading a script. Try a concise initial explanation; expand when the next question asks for detail.
3. Ask one follow-up based on a specific claim in that answer. If you do not know something, identify what needs checking.
4. Review the exchange. Separate factual inconsistencies, missing explanations and delivery habits such as rushing.
5. Resolve one gap and repeat with a differently worded question. Do not rehearse until an invented answer sounds fluent.

You can use a private recording, a friend or [application-based AI practice](/visa-types/b1-b2). A practice timer helps you notice rambling; it does not reproduce an official interview duration.

## Before you finish: check the records behind the answers

Review relevant dates, who is paying, accommodation and your employment or study history. A [document check](/document-checks) can help you notice differences across selected records, while [interview feedback](/#interview-example) helps identify a detail left unexplained in speech. A matching document or a fluent answer does not guarantee eligibility.

For appointment logistics, follow your post's own instructions.
