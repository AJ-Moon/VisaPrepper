Bangladeshi applicants for U.S. visas span a wide range of categories — from students applying to American universities, to families reuniting with relatives, to businesspeople and exchange visitors. This guide focuses on the categories and themes most relevant if you're preparing for an interview from Bangladesh, rather than a generic list written for no country in particular.

## Where interviews happen

Visa interviews for Bangladeshi applicants — both nonimmigrant and immigrant categories — are conducted at the U.S. Embassy in Dhaka. Applicants often travel from Chattogram, Sylhet, Khulna, and other cities for their appointment, so it's worth confirming your appointment details, required documents, visa fee payment steps, and any current entry procedures well in advance rather than the day before. Appointment availability and wait times can shift over the year, so build in buffer time if your travel or program dates are fixed.

## F1 student visas from Bangladesh

Bangladesh has a growing number of students applying to U.S. universities and colleges each year, often after completing HSC or an undergraduate degree at home, or as graduate applicants moving directly into a master's program. Common interview focus areas include:

- A clear, specific reason for choosing your university and program — not just its ranking
- Detailed, credible funding — often a combination of family support, savings, and increasingly education loans or partial scholarships and assistantships
- Your academic background and how this program logically connects to your goals
- Your plans after graduation, and your ties to Bangladesh

If your funding involves multiple sources — for example, a family contribution plus a bank loan plus a partial scholarship — practice explaining how the pieces fit together and cover your full costs, not just naming each source individually.

See our complete [F1 visa interview guide](/blog/f1-visa-interview-questions-and-answers) for practice questions and common mistakes to avoid.

## B1/B2 visitor visas from Bangladesh

Family visits are especially common among Bangladeshi B1/B2 applicants, given the size of the Bangladeshi-American community, alongside business travel and occasional tourism. Business travel often comes from Bangladesh's garment and textile export sector, along with other trade-related fields — if that's your situation, be ready to explain your company, your role, and the specific purpose of your U.S. meetings clearly.

Focus especially on:

- A specific, believable trip purpose — who you're visiting, for how long, and why now
- Clear ties to home — your job, business, family responsibilities, or ongoing studies in Bangladesh
- Consistency between your stated funding source and your actual financial situation, including any support from relatives abroad
- For business travelers, a clear explanation of your company, your buyers or partners in the U.S., and the purpose of your specific trip

See our complete [B1/B2 visa interview guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) for more detail, and our guide on [214(b) refusals](/blog/214b-visa-refusal-guide) if you're reapplying after a previous refusal.

## Family-based immigrant visas from Bangladesh

A significant number of Bangladeshi applicants apply through family-based immigrant petitions — spouses, parents, children, or siblings of U.S. citizens or permanent residents, reflecting a well-established diaspora particularly in New York and other major U.S. cities. These cases typically start with an approved petition (commonly Form I-130), move through National Visa Center processing, and end with a consular interview in Dhaka that focuses heavily on relationship history and consistency with the underlying petition.

Because these cases can take a long time to reach the interview stage, applicants sometimes find that details from the original petition feel distant by interview day. It's worth revisiting your own petition paperwork shortly before your appointment so you can speak about it confidently, not just recall it vaguely from years earlier.

## Work and exchange visas

While smaller in volume than F1 or family-based categories, Bangladeshi applicants also apply for H1B work visas — often in technology, engineering, and healthcare fields — and J1 exchange programs, including research and training placements. ## Practice questions across the most common categories

**If you're a student:**

1. Why did you choose this university and program?
2. How will you fund your studies, and who is supporting you?
3. What are your plans after graduation?

**If you're visiting family or on business:**

1. What is the purpose of your trip, and how long will you stay?
2. Who is paying for your trip, and how?
3. What ties you to Bangladesh — your job, business, or family?

**If you're applying through a family petition:**

1. How are you related to the person who petitioned for you?
2. When did your petition process begin, and what's happened since?
3. Can you describe your relationship history and any time you've spent together?

Practice these out loud in your own words, based on your real situation — not as a script to recite.

## Before you travel to Dhaka for your appointment

If you're traveling from outside Dhaka, plan for more than just the interview slot itself. Confirm your visa fee payment method and location, gather your documents a few days ahead rather than the night before, and allow extra travel time in case of transport delays — arriving rushed and stressed rarely helps an applicant present their case clearly, however well-prepared they otherwise are.

## Common preparation gaps worth addressing

A few patterns come up often enough among Bangladeshi applicants to call out directly:

- **Funding that relies heavily on relatives abroad.** This is completely normal, but explain it specifically — who is sending support, how regularly, and how it fits with your own savings or income — rather than describing it in general terms.
- **Long gaps between petition filing and interview** for family-based cases, which can leave details feeling less familiar than they should by interview day.
- **Business ties described too generally.** "I do business" is weaker than naming your company, your role, and a specific, current reason for your U.S. trip.

## Preparing in Bengali — or your own language

Interviews are conducted in English, but thinking through your answers in Bengali first is completely normal for many applicants. What matters is a clear, honest, well-understood explanation — not native-level English fluency. If English isn't your strongest language, focus your practice on saying the key facts simply and clearly rather than constructing long, complex sentences.
