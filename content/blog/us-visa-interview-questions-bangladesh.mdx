Bangladeshi applicants for U.S. visas span a wide range of categories — from students applying to American universities, to families reuniting with relatives, to businesspeople and exchange visitors. This guide focuses on the categories and themes most relevant if you're preparing for an interview from Bangladesh.

## Where interviews happen

Visa interviews for Bangladeshi applicants — both nonimmigrant and immigrant categories — are conducted at the U.S. Embassy in Dhaka. Applicants often travel from across the country for their appointment, so it's worth confirming your appointment details, required documents, and any current entry procedures well in advance rather than the day before.

## F1 student visas from Bangladesh

Bangladesh has a growing number of students applying to U.S. universities and colleges each year. Common interview focus areas include:

- A clear, specific reason for choosing your university and program
- Detailed, credible funding — often a combination of family support and, increasingly, education loans or partial scholarships
- Your academic background and how this program fits your goals

See our complete [F1 visa interview guide](/blog/f1-visa-interview-questions-and-answers) for practice questions and common mistakes to avoid.

## B1/B2 visitor visas from Bangladesh

Family visits are especially common among Bangladeshi B1/B2 applicants, given the size of the Bangladeshi-American community, alongside business travel and occasional tourism.

Focus especially on:

- A specific, believable trip purpose — who you're visiting, for how long, and why now
- Clear ties to home — your job, business, family responsibilities, or ongoing studies in Bangladesh
- Consistency between your stated funding source and your actual financial situation

See our complete [B1/B2 visa interview guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) for more detail, and our guide on [214(b) refusals](/blog/214b-visa-refusal-guide) if you're reapplying after a previous refusal.

## Family-based immigrant visas from Bangladesh

A significant number of Bangladeshi applicants apply through family-based immigrant petitions — spouses, parents, children, or siblings of U.S. citizens or permanent residents, reflecting a well-established diaspora particularly in New York and other major U.S. cities. These interviews focus heavily on relationship history and consistency with the underlying petition.

See our [family and immigrant visa page](/visa-types/family-immigrant) for the topics these interviews typically cover.

## Work and exchange visas

While smaller in volume than F1 or family-based categories, Bangladeshi applicants also apply for H1B work visas and J1 exchange programs. See our [H1B visa interview guide](/blog/h1b-visa-interview-questions) and [J1 visa interview guide](/blog/j1-visa-interview-questions) if either applies to you.

## Preparing in Bengali — or your own language

Interviews are conducted in English, but thinking through your answers in Bengali first is completely normal for many applicants. What matters is a clear, honest, well-understood explanation — not native-level English fluency.
