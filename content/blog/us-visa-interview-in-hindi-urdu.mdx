Searching for US visa interview questions in Hindi usually means one of two things. You want to know whether you can answer the officer in Hindi or Urdu, or you want to practice in the language you think in before the real interview. This guide covers both, using the official U.S. government sources listed at the end, and gives illustrative prompts in Hindi and Urdu with English equivalents for visitor, student and H-1B/H-4 interviews.

The short answer: do not assume you can choose. None of the official pages we checked promises a Hindi or Urdu interview for visitor, student or work visas. Some appointment systems may ask for a preferred interview language when you book. If yours does, choose the language you can answer in comfortably and check that it appears on your confirmation; do not count on an interpreter being arranged on the day. Either way, prepare to explain your facts in English and in the language you are most likely to use.

## Can the U.S. visa interview be in Hindi or Urdu?

What official sources say, and what they leave open. The travel.state.gov pages below blocked automated access on 21 November 2026, so we describe the latest versions we could read: New Delhi (marked last updated 4 June 2026), Mumbai (9 July 2026) and Islamabad (19 December 2025). Confirm them on the live pages.

| Official source | What it says | What it does not tell you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [U.S. Embassy New Delhi: immigrant visa interview instructions](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Supplements/Supplements_by_Post/NWD-New-Delhi.html) | Applicants may bring one interpreter if they do not speak English, Hindi, Punjabi, Malayalam, Tibetan or Tamil well enough to take part in the interview. | It covers immigrant visa interviews only, not B1/B2, F-1 or H-1B interviews. |
| [U.S. Consulate General Mumbai: immigrant visa interview instructions](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Supplements/Supplements_by_Post/BMB-Mumbai.html) | The only person listed as able to accompany an applicant is one helper for someone elderly, disabled or under 18. Attorneys may not come in, and there is no interpreter provision. | Also immigrant visas only. It does not say which languages officers use. |
| [U.S. Embassy Islamabad: immigrant visa interview instructions](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Supplements/Supplements_by_Post/ISL-Islamabad.html) | Applicants who do not speak English or Urdu well enough to take part may bring one interpreter. | Also immigrant visas only. It does not promise an Urdu interview to anyone. |
| [Foreign Affairs Manual guidance on student visas](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM040205.html) | If a student's Form I-20 shows English is required for the course and no arrangement has been made to address a shortfall, the officer must assess English ability and conduct the interview in English. | It covers that student situation, not every applicant. |

Our reading, not a stated policy: because applicants who speak Hindi (New Delhi) or Urdu (Islamabad) are not told to bring an interpreter, those posts appear able to hold immigrant visa interviews in those languages. Neither page says so directly, and we found no India or Pakistan page that lists interview languages for nonimmigrant visas.

### F-1 students: prepare in English

The same Foreign Affairs Manual section says the officer may ask a student to read aloud from an English-language text and restate it in their own words, or to read and explain conditions on the Form I-20. A student must show English ability only when the school has made it a requirement for the course. Officers must not second-guess a school that admitted you on TOEFL, IELTS or similar scores unless fraud is suspected, and English ability is not required if your school has arranged English study first or teaches your course in another language you speak well.

If you will study in English, treat English as your main interview language. Hindi or Urdu practice still helps you sort out your funding and study plan before you explain them in English. Our [F1 interview questions guide](/blog/f1-visa-interview-questions-and-answers) covers the topics.

### Can I bring a translator or interpreter?

The interpreter rules above are for immigrant visas only. For a visitor, student or work visa interview, do not plan to bring a relative or agent to interpret unless your appointment instructions allow it; if you are unsure, use the contact options on the official [U.S. Mission India visa page](https://in.usembassy.gov/visas/) or [U.S. Mission Pakistan visa page](https://pk.usembassy.gov/visas/) well before interview day.

Accessibility is separate. The [India](https://in.usembassy.gov/visas/important-visa-information/) and [Pakistan](https://pk.usembassy.gov/visas/important-visa-information/) missions' visa information pages say an applicant who is deaf or hard of hearing may bring a sign language interpreter if needed. They list this under reasonable accommodation and ask applicants to request it as soon as they schedule their appointment.

### If Hindi or Urdu is not your strongest language

Many applicants think most easily in Punjabi, Telugu, Gujarati, Tamil or another language. Organize your facts in your strongest language, then practice saying them in English and, if it suits you, Hindi or Urdu.

## Your DS-160 is in English: keep both languages consistent

The State Department's [DS-160 FAQ](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/forms/ds-160-online-nonimmigrant-visa-application/ds-160-faqs.html) says answers must be in English, using English characters, except where the form asks for your full name in your native alphabet. Translated question text is available from a language menu, but your answers stay in English. (Immigrant visa applicants complete the DS-260 instead; the same consistency advice applies.)

So if you speak Hindi or Urdu at the interview, you are explaining an English record in another language. That is where small mismatches creep in.

| Detail | How it can slip between languages | What to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family relationships | Everyday Hindi and Urdu often call a cousin *bhai* or *behan*, and use separate words for each kind of uncle and aunt. | Use the English relationship on your form: "my cousin", "my mother's brother". If the form says cousin, do not call them your brother in either language. |
| Amounts | Families discuss budgets in lakh and crore; documents may show full rupee figures or U.S. dollars. | Write each amount both ways: 20 lakh rupees is 2,000,000 rupees. Know the dollar figures as they appear on your I-20 or trip budget. |
| Job title | "Office work" or "business" can sound different from the title on your form. | Learn the English title you entered and describe two real daily tasks in each language. |
| Length of stay | "Two or three months" in conversation can conflict with the stay you entered. | Know the intended length on your form and the reason for it. |
| Sponsor | "Papa will manage" or "the family is paying" hides who actually pays. | Name the person, their relationship to you and what they cover. |
| Names | University, employer and hotel names get shortened or translated in speech. | Say proper names exactly as written in your documents. |

If a DS-160 answer is wrong, do not memorize the error just to stay consistent. You generally can't edit a DS-160 after you submit it; follow your embassy's instructions, which often means completing a new DS-160 and updating your appointment with the new confirmation number. Our [DS-160 review guide](/blog/ds-160-interview-consistency) explains what to check. For supporting documents in Hindi or Urdu, the New Delhi, Mumbai and Islamabad immigrant visa instructions ask for English translations; other categories follow their own document lists.

## US visa interview questions in Hindi and Urdu: practice prompts

**Example (illustrative):** these are common preparation topics written as practice prompts, not an official or predicted question list. Each prompt appears in English, then Hindi (Devanagari), then Urdu, with a note on what it checks. Answer only from your own facts. For the full question set by visa type, see our [US visa interview questions hub](/blog/us-visa-interview-questions).

### B1/B2 visitors

**1. Purpose.** Why do you want to go to the United States?

<p lang="hi">आप अमेरिका क्यों जाना चाहते हैं?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ امریکہ کیوں جانا چاہتے ہیں؟</p>

*Checks:* that your reason fits a visitor visa and matches your DS-160.

**2. Funding.** Who will pay for your trip?

<p lang="hi">आपकी यात्रा का खर्च कौन उठाएगा?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ کے سفر کا خرچ کون اٹھائے گا؟</p>

*Checks:* who pays, their relationship to you and that the money is available.

**3. Stay.** How long will you stay, and where?

<p lang="hi">आप कितने समय के लिए और कहाँ रुकेंगे?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ کتنے عرصے کے لیے اور کہاں ٹھہریں گے؟</p>

*Checks:* your dates and address against your form, and what you return to afterwards.

**4. Work.** What work do you do?

<p lang="hi">आप क्या काम करते हैं?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ کیا کام کرتے ہیں؟</p>

*Checks:* your current job title and duties as entered on your form.

**5. Family in the U.S.** Who is your relative in the United States, and what do they do?

<p lang="hi">अमेरिका में आपके रिश्तेदार कौन हैं, और वे क्या करते हैं?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">امریکہ میں آپ کے رشتہ دار کون ہیں، اور وہ کیا کرتے ہیں؟</p>

*Checks:* the relationship exactly as written on your form.

**6. Travel history.** Have you travelled abroad before, and has a U.S. visa ever been refused?

<p lang="hi">क्या आप पहले विदेश जा चुके हैं? क्या कभी आपका अमेरिकी वीज़ा अस्वीकार हुआ है?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">کیا آپ پہلے بیرونِ ملک جا چکے ہیں؟ کیا کبھی آپ کا امریکی ویزا مسترد ہوا ہے؟</p>

*Checks:* your travel and refusal history against your DS-160.

Our [B1/B2 interview questions guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) covers follow-ups for visitors.

### F-1 students

**7. University.** Why did you choose this university?

<p lang="hi">आपने यही यूनिवर्सिटी क्यों चुनी?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ نے یہی یونیورسٹی کیوں منتخب کی؟</p>

*Checks:* that your choice of school and course is genuine and that you can explain it.

**8. Sponsor.** Who will pay for your studies, and what do they do?

<p lang="hi">आपकी पढ़ाई का खर्च कौन उठाएगा, और वे क्या काम करते हैं?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ کی تعلیم کا خرچ کون اٹھائے گا، اور وہ کیا کام کرتے ہیں؟</p>

*Checks:* that your funding matches your I-20 and your sponsor's real income.

**9. After graduation.** What will you do after you complete your degree?

<p lang="hi">डिग्री पूरी होने के बाद आप क्या करेंगे?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">ڈگری مکمل ہونے کے بعد آپ کیا کریں گے؟</p>

*Checks:* that your plans after study are clear and fit a temporary student visa.

### H-1B and H-4

**10. Employer and role.** Which company will you work for in the United States, and what will your role be?

<p lang="hi">अमेरिका में आप किस कंपनी के लिए काम करेंगे, और आपका पद क्या होगा?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">امریکہ میں آپ کس کمپنی کے لیے کام کریں گے، اور آپ کا عہدہ کیا ہوگا؟</p>

*Checks:* your employer, job title and duties against your petition.

**11. Worksite.** Where will you actually work: at your employer's office or at a client site?

<p lang="hi">आप असल में कहाँ काम करेंगे, अपनी कंपनी के दफ़्तर में या किसी क्लाइंट की साइट पर?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ اصل میں کہاں کام کریں گے، اپنی کمپنی کے دفتر میں یا کسی کلائنٹ کی سائٹ پر؟</p>

*Checks:* where, and for whom, the work really happens.

**12. H-4 marriage.** When did you get married, and what does your spouse do in the United States?

<p lang="hi">आपकी शादी कब हुई, और आपके जीवनसाथी अमेरिका में क्या काम करते हैं?</p>

<p lang="ur" dir="rtl">آپ کی شادی کب ہوئی، اور آپ کے شریکِ حیات امریکہ میں کیا کام کرتے ہیں؟</p>

*Checks:* your marriage details and your spouse's job against your documents.

Our [H1B](/blog/h1b-visa-interview-questions) and [H4](/blog/h4-visa-interview-questions) guides go deeper; spouses immigrating to the U.S. can use our [IR1/CR1 guide](/blog/spouse-visa-interview-questions-ir1-cr1).

**How to use them:** a partner reads the Hindi or Urdu prompt and you answer in English. Next round, they ask in English and you answer in Hindi or Urdu. If a number, name or date changes between rounds, check it against your documents before the next session.

## Build an answer you can say in either language

Short, factual answers are easier to keep identical across two languages. Use three parts:

1. **Direct answer:** one sentence that answers the question actually asked.
2. **One checkable fact:** a name, amount, date or role you can find in your documents.
3. **Stop:** let the officer ask the next question instead of adding a speech.

**Example (illustrative):** a parent visiting a son in the U.S. is asked who will pay.

- Unclear: "My son is there, he will manage everything."
- Clearer: "My son is paying for my flight, and I will stay at his home. I will use my pension for my own expenses."

Use this structure only with your true details. Parents preparing for this kind of trip can continue with our [B1/B2 guide for parents](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions-for-parents).

Mixing languages is normal. Many people speak Hindi or Urdu with English words for jobs, courses and money; keep those words exactly as they appear on your documents. If you do not understand a question, ask for it to be repeated rather than guessing. Our [guide for nervous applicants](/blog/visa-interview-nervous-forget-answer) has a short routine for that moment.

## A language checklist before interview day

- If your booking asks for an interview language, choose the one you answer in most comfortably and check it on your confirmation.
- Know your DS-160 answers in English, especially relationships, job title, sponsor and intended stay.
- Write key amounts in lakh or crore, as full figures, and in dollars where relevant.
- Students: practice your course, funding and plans in English.
- Practice each core prompt once in English and once in Hindi or Urdu.
- Read your post's rules on who may accompany you, and do not plan to bring an interpreter unless they allow it.
- Review the steps of the day itself in our [interview day guide](/blog/us-visa-interview-day-what-to-expect).
