A **214(b) refusal** means the visa authority did not find that the applicant met the applicable standard for that nonimmigrant visa application. Before planning another interview, read the refusal notice, identify the relevant visa category and consult the current official instructions. Better preparation can help you explain your facts; it does not change the legal standard or guarantee another outcome.

## What Section 214(b) covers

The [Department of State's visa-denials guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html) distinguishes failure to establish eligibility for a category from failure to overcome the presumption of immigrant intent where that applies. The immigrant-intent requirement has exceptions, including H-1B and L applicants and specified family members. Do not transfer a visitor-style promise to return home to every visa category.

A 214(b) refusal is specific to that application and has no appeal process. The official guidance explains reapplication when relevant additional information or significant changes in circumstances can be presented. Follow the current procedure for a new application rather than treating a practice session as a way to reverse a decision.

## 214(b) and 221(g) are different

A 221(g) refusal can involve information, documents or administrative processing still needed before eligibility can be determined. It is still a refusal at that point. Follow the notice's instructions; do not substitute general reapplication advice for a request for a specific document or an administrative-processing update. Read the [official explanation of both refusal categories](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html).

## Separate what you know from what you are guessing

A short interview can leave you uncertain about which fact needed attention. Write three lists for your own preparation:

- **The notice:** its actual refusal category and any instructions you were given.
- **Your records:** what the application, travel or study plan, and supporting evidence actually state.
- **Questions to clarify:** details you did not explain clearly or could not verify afterward.

Do not invent an officer's reason from a facial expression, interview length or a comment on social media. If you need advice about eligibility, reapplication or the legal implications of your history, seek a qualified immigration professional.

## Review the details for your own category

For a visitor or business trip, review the actual purpose, length of stay, costs and plans afterward. Our [B1/B2 question guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) has separate family-visit and business exercises.

For an F1 study plan, understand the course, why it fits your background and how the costs will be covered. Use the [F1 question guide](/blog/f1-visa-interview-questions-and-answers) and [funding preparation exercise](/blog/f1-visa-interview-who-will-pay). Do not assume that every student refusal was caused by a vague funding answer.

For other categories, start with the requirements and purpose of that category. A [document checklist](/blog/us-visa-interview-documents-checklist) helps organize records; a [consistency check](/document-checks) can compare selected details but cannot determine why a visa was refused.

## Practice the next detail, not a new story

A reasonable first answer may leave something important unexplained. In our [illustrative interview report](/#interview-example), a fictional applicant names a sponsor and savings but does not explain how the available funds compare with costs. The preparation task is to review actual amounts and sources, then explain the arrangement truthfully. It is not a claim about approval, available money or a real customer's reapplication.

Try one question out loud, then ask a friend which detail a follow-up would need. Keep the application and supporting records nearby for checking afterward. If information has genuinely changed, distinguish the earlier fact from the current one and follow the proper correction or disclosure instructions. Do not silently rewrite your history or memorize a more persuasive version.

## What an interview-practice tool can and cannot do

Application-based practice can ask follow-up questions and help you notice unclear explanations or inconsistent details. It cannot diagnose the legal reason behind a refusal, predict the officer's next questions or promise a different result.
