H4 interviews put many applicants in an unfamiliar position: the questions are often as much about your spouse's job as they are about you. That can feel disorienting if you've mostly prepared around your own background, career, or studies. This guide covers what H4 interviews typically focus on, how children and spouses are each assessed, and how to prepare without over-relying on someone else's answers.

## What an H4 interview is actually checking

H4 status is for the spouse and unmarried children under 21 of an H1B visa holder (the "principal applicant"). Officers are generally confirming three things: that your relationship to the principal applicant is genuine and properly documented, that you understand the basic shape of their H1B status, and — if you're applying together or around the same time — that your answers and your spouse's answers are consistent with each other.

Because H4 is a dependent category, your case is legally tied to your spouse's H1B approval. If there are questions about their status, those can affect your interview too, which is part of why understanding their situation, even at a basic level, matters more here than it would in most other visa categories.

In practice, H4 interviews are often shorter than the H1B interview itself, which can tempt applicants into under-preparing. That's a mistake worth avoiding: a short interview still needs clear, confident answers, and a hesitant or confused response in a brief window can stand out more, not less.

## Common H4 interview topics

### Your relationship

Practice questions:

- How did you meet your spouse?
- When and where did you get married?
- Did you have an engagement period, and how long was it?
- Do you have children together, and are they applying with you?

Know your own relationship history well — dates, key milestones, and how the relationship developed — described naturally rather than rehearsed. If your marriage was arranged, introduced through family, or happened quickly, that's completely normal to explain honestly; officers are used to a wide range of genuine relationship stories and aren't looking for a particular "type" of courtship.

### Your principal applicant's status

Practice questions:

- What does your spouse do for work?
- Who is their employer, and roughly what industry are they in?
- How long have they held H1B status, and is this their first employer on H1B?
- Where will you be living in the United States?

You're not expected to know every detail of their job, but basic familiarity — their employer's name, general industry, and role — matters. If you genuinely don't know something, it's better to say so plainly than to guess.

If your spouse changed employers recently, or is on a relatively new H1B approval, it's worth confirming the current details with them beforehand rather than describing an outdated job. This is a common, easily avoidable source of inconsistency — not because anyone is being dishonest, but because H4 applicants sometimes describe a previous role out of habit.

### Your plans in the United States

Practice questions:

- What will you do while your spouse is working?
- Do you plan to study, if you're eligible to?
- Are you planning to apply for work authorization, and do you understand the current rules that apply to your situation?
- Who will care for any children, and what are the arrangements?

Rules around H4 work authorization (H4 EAD eligibility) have changed over time and depend on your spouse's specific H1B history. Rather than asserting a specific legal position in the interview, it's safer to describe your general plans and note that you'll follow the applicable process if and when you're eligible.

### Children applying as H4 dependents

If your children are applying alongside you, expect questions confirming their relationship to the principal applicant (usually straightforward with a birth certificate) and simple, age-appropriate questions about schooling plans. Officers generally aren't looking for children to answer complex questions — the scrutiny here is mostly about documentation, not interview performance.

### Consistency between spouses

If you and your spouse are both interviewing — sometimes on the same day, sometimes separately, and sometimes in different countries if you're not currently living together — your answers about your relationship, your living situation, and your plans should naturally align. That doesn't mean identical, word-for-word answers; it means your answers should describe the same true situation without significant contradictions on core facts.

It's common for one spouse to already be in the U.S. on H1B status while the other applies for H4 later, sometimes months apart. If that's your situation, be ready to explain the timeline plainly — when your spouse moved, when you plan to join them, and what's kept you apart in the meantime (finishing a degree, a notice period at work, waiting for this specific appointment).

## Documents worth reviewing beforehand

- **Marriage certificate**, and for children, birth certificates — this is the core evidence of your relationship to the principal applicant.
- **Your spouse's H1B approval notice (Form I-797)** and supporting petition details — you don't need the full petition memorized, but you should recognize it and know roughly what it says about their role and employer.
- **Evidence of your relationship timeline**, if relevant to your specific case — photos, correspondence, or joint records that support a genuine, ongoing relationship.
- **Your own passport and DS-160 confirmation**, reviewed for consistency with what you plan to say about your relationship and plans.

## Sample H4 interview questions to practice with

1. How and when did you meet your spouse?
2. When did you get married, and where?
3. What does your spouse do, and for which company?
4. How long has your spouse held H1B status?
5. Do you have children? Are they applying with you?
6. What will you be doing while in the United States?
7. Where will you be living?

## Mistakes that weaken H4 interview answers

- **Not knowing basic facts about the principal applicant's job.** A vague "I don't really know what he does" can raise questions, even in a genuine relationship — a one-sentence description of their role and employer is usually enough.
- **Inconsistent relationship timeline.** Dates and key details about your relationship history should be consistent with what's in your application and with what your spouse says.
- **Treating it like a solo interview.** Unlike F1 or B1/B2, your case is directly tied to another person's status — prepare with that connection in mind, rather than only reviewing your own background.
- **Overstating work authorization plans.** Speaking as if H4 work authorization is automatically guaranteed, when eligibility depends on your spouse's specific H1B history and current rules, can come across as unprepared if the officer asks a follow-up.
- **Describing an outdated job or employer.** If your spouse recently changed roles, make sure you're describing their current position, not the one they held when you last discussed it in detail.
- **Under-preparing because "it's my spouse's visa."** H4 is its own visa category with its own interview — showing up without having thought through your own answers, assuming the interview will be brief and easy, is a common and avoidable mistake.
