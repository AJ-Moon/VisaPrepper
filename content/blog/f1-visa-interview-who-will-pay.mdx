“Who will pay for your studies?” sounds simple. But naming one person may leave the funding plan unclear.

Before you practise, understand where the money comes from and what it covers. Do not copy another student's answer.

## Make one clear funding note

Using your real documents, write down:

- Your course costs and the period they cover.
- Each confirmed source of support.
- The amount available from each source.
- Any costs that still need funding.

Keep tuition separate from living and travel costs. Label every amount with its currency. Do not mix a monthly income with a full year's costs.

The [Department of State's student visa guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/student-visa.html) explains that an officer may ask how educational, living, and travel costs will be paid. There is no universal bank-balance target in this article.

## If a parent or relative is helping

Practise explaining who they are and what support is actually available. Understand the arrangement yourself.

A useful set of practice questions is:

1. Who is helping you?
2. What do they do?
3. What costs will their support cover?
4. Which records show the support?
5. Is anyone else contributing?

You do not need to invent a successful business or a larger income. An impressive story is not a substitute for correct information.

## If you have an education loan

Read the actual loan documents. Know whether the loan is approved, what conditions apply, and what it covers.

For an applicant in India, a useful private exercise is to separate INR loan amounts from USD course costs. Do not treat an enquiry or an application as an approved loan.

If you do not understand a condition, ask the lender. An interview practice tool cannot confirm that funds will be released.

## If you have a scholarship

Check the award, not just the headline amount. Does it cover tuition only? Is it for one year or a longer period? What costs remain?

For an applicant in Bangladesh, separate BDT family support from USD scholarship figures. For an applicant in Pakistan, do the same with PKR and USD.

These are organization exercises. They are not different visa rules for each nationality.

## If several people or sources are involved

Use one line per source. This helps you avoid counting the same savings twice.

Then explain the plan without reading your note. If you keep changing which person pays which cost, check the arrangement before trying again.

Our [DS-160 guide](/blog/ds-160-interview-consistency) explains why your form and spoken answer should describe the same true information. If the form is wrong, follow official correction instructions. Do not hide the difference.

## Try two follow-up questions

After your first explanation, practise:

- “What does the scholarship not cover?”
- “How will you pay the remaining costs?”

Only use questions that fit your real situation. If a source is not confirmed, say so honestly and check what that means for your application.

## What useful feedback should help you notice

Look for a missing amount, an unclear source, mixed currencies, or an answer that changes between questions. The goal is to understand the plan, not learn a convincing line.
