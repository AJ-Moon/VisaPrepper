Searching for **documents required for a U.S. visa interview**? Start with your visa category and official appointment instructions. A student in Pakistan, a visitor in India, and a family-based immigrant applicant in Bangladesh should not pack from one universal list.

Use this guide to organize your preparation. It is not a complete or official document checklist.

## First, identify the right checklist

Write your visa category at the top of your notes: for example, F1 for study or B1/B2 for a temporary visit. Family-based immigrant cases have a separate process—do not assume the nonimmigrant DS-160 checklist applies to them.

Then open your official appointment instructions. Make three groups: **required items, case-specific supporting records, and things to clarify**. Do not replace a requirement with advice from social media.

## Nonimmigrant interview basics

For student and visitor applications, review the current official instructions covering your passport, DS-160 confirmation, fee receipt where required, and photo requirements. Keep your appointment confirmation easy to find.

The [official visitor visa checklist](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) and [student visa checklist](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/student-visa.html) explain the requirements and exceptions. A photo upload failure, for example, changes what you need to bring.

For the DS-160, see our [form and confirmation page guide](/blog/ds-160-interview-consistency). Keep the application confirmation separate from the appointment record.

## Student documents: connect funding to the course

For F1 preparation, start with your Form I-20 and the official student checklist. Review your academic and funding records so you understand your course, its cost, and how you will pay.

For your own review:

- Separate a scholarship award from an application for a scholarship.
- Distinguish an approved education loan from an estimate or enquiry.
- Check what family support covers and what it does not.
- Label local-currency figures and U.S.-dollar figures clearly.
- Do not treat the same money as two separate funding sources.

These are consistency checks, not rules about a minimum bank balance. There is no single balance figure supplied by this guide. Practice with our [F1 interview questions](/blog/f1-visa-interview-questions-and-answers).

## Visitor documents: purpose, costs and plans afterward

Review the records relevant to your actual visit. Your preparation should help you explain why you are travelling, how costs will be covered, and what your plans are after the visit.

For a parent visiting family, separate the relative's offer of accommodation from any offer to pay for flights. For a business trip, understand the event or meeting and your role in it.

The [Department of State](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) says a visitor invitation letter is not required and does not determine whether a visa is issued. Do not buy a letter or document package believing it guarantees a visa.

Use our [visitor interview guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) to practice explaining the details.

## Work, dependent, exchange and immigrant cases

Use the official checklist for your case before deciding what to bring. To prepare your explanations, choose the relevant guide:

- [H1B work interviews](/blog/h1b-visa-interview-questions): understand your employer and role.
- [H4 dependent interviews](/blog/h4-visa-interview-questions): review the relationship and principal applicant's details.
- [J1 exchange interviews](/blog/j1-visa-interview-questions): understand your program and funding.
- [Family-based immigrant preparation](/visa-types/family-immigrant): review the case-specific relationship and document instructions.

Medical, civil-document, translation and other requirements can differ. A student checklist is not a substitute for immigrant visa instructions.

## Local preparation: Pakistan, India and Bangladesh

**Pakistan:** if a relative or agent organized your file, review it yourself. Check names, dates and funding explanations. Use the [Pakistan guide](/blog/us-visa-interview-questions-pakistan) for English and Urdu practice tips.

**India:** keep education-loan and scholarship records distinct. If you have separate appointment confirmations, check each one's instructions. See the [India guide](/blog/us-visa-interview-questions-india).

**Bangladesh:** check how names and abbreviations appear across your records, and what the official checklist says about translations. Keep BDT and USD amounts labelled. See the [Bangladesh guide](/blog/us-visa-interview-questions-bangladesh).

Do not change original documents to remove a mismatch. Find the reason and follow the appropriate official correction process.

## Pack in a way you can use

1. Put the required appointment and identity papers where you can reach them.
2. Group supporting records by purpose: study, money, work, or family.
3. Keep originals and copies clearly separated.
4. Note any unresolved question and check it before the appointment.
5. Practice explaining a document without reading a long paragraph from it.

A neat folder helps you find things; it does not affect eligibility by itself. Follow your appointment's rules about permitted bags and items.
