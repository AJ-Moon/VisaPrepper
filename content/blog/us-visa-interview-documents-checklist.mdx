Interview day nerves get worse when you're also unsure whether you have the right paperwork. This checklist separates the documents almost every applicant should review from the ones that depend on your specific visa type — so you can prepare calmly instead of guessing.

Requirements can vary by consulate, by visa category, and over time, so treat this as a starting point for organizing your own preparation, not a substitute for your appointment confirmation letter or official guidance.

## Why documents matter beyond just "having" them

Consular officers generally aren't examining every page of every document in detail during a short interview — what they're really assessing is whether your spoken answers match the paper trail you've already submitted. That means the real value of this checklist isn't collecting paperwork to hand over; it's making sure you understand what each document says well enough to talk about it naturally, so nothing in your file surprises you if it comes up.

## Universal documents most applicants should review

- **A valid passport** with enough validity period remaining, as specified by your visa category
- **Your DS-160 confirmation page**, printed or saved, along with a copy of the form's answers
- **Your interview appointment confirmation letter**
- **Visa application fee payment receipt**
- **A recent passport-style photo**, if your appointment location requires you to bring one separately
- **Any documents specific to a previous visa refusal**, if applicable, so you can reference what changed

Review [DS-160 consistency](/blog/ds-160-interview-consistency) as part of this step — the goal isn't just having the paperwork, it's understanding it well enough to answer questions about it confidently.

## Visa-specific documents to review

### F1 student visa

- Form I-20, signed by both you and your school's designated school official
- Evidence of SEVIS fee payment
- Financial documents supporting your funding — bank statements, sponsor letters, scholarship or assistantship documentation
- Academic records: transcripts, degree certificates, standardized test scores if applicable

Your I-20 is the single most important document in an F1 interview — it lists your school, program, and the funding amount your school expects you to demonstrate, so your financial documents should clearly support that specific figure, not just show "enough money" in general terms.

See our [F1 visa interview guide](/blog/f1-visa-interview-questions-and-answers) for the topics these documents typically support.

### B1/B2 tourist and business visa

- Evidence of purpose of travel — invitation letters, conference registration, or a trip itinerary, where relevant
- Evidence of ties to home — employment letter, business registration, or property documents
- Financial evidence supporting the cost of the trip
- For business travel: a letter from your employer describing the purpose of your trip

Given how short B1/B2 interviews typically are, having your ties-to-home evidence organized and top of mind — rather than buried at the bottom of a folder — can matter more here than in longer interview categories.

See our [B1/B2 visa interview guide](/blog/b1-b2-visa-interview-questions) for common topics tied to these documents.

### H1B work visa

- Approval notice for your H1B petition (Form I-797)
- A copy of your Form I-129 petition and Labor Condition Application (LCA), if available to you
- Recent pay stubs or employment verification, if you're renewing or extending status
- Educational credentials relevant to your specialty occupation

See our [H1B visa interview guide](/blog/h1b-visa-interview-questions) for how these connect to common interview questions.

### H4 dependent visa

- Marriage certificate or birth certificate establishing your relationship to the principal applicant
- The principal applicant's H1B approval notice and supporting documents
- Evidence of the relationship's timeline, where relevant

See our [H4 visa interview guide](/blog/h4-visa-interview-questions) for typical topics.

### J1 exchange visitor visa

- Form DS-2019 issued by your program sponsor
- Evidence of SEVIS fee payment
- Program-specific documentation from your sponsor organization
- Financial evidence supporting your participation, including any stipend details

See our [J1 visa interview guide](/blog/j1-visa-interview-questions) for common program-related questions.

### Family-based immigrant visa

- The approved immigrant petition (such as Form I-130) and receipt notices
- Civil documents establishing your relationship — marriage certificate, birth certificates
- Form I-864 Affidavit of Support and related financial documents, if applicable to your case
- Evidence supporting a genuine relationship, for spouse-based cases

See our [family and immigrant visa page](/visa-types/family-immigrant) and our guide on [preparing after a previous refusal](/blog/214b-visa-refusal-guide) if relevant to your case.

## Organizing your documents well

How you organize your paperwork won't change a visa decision on its own, but it does affect how smoothly your interview goes, and a calmer interview experience generally helps you present your case clearly:

- Keep original documents together in one folder, in a logical order matching how they're likely to come up (identity documents first, then financial, then visa-specific).
- Keep photocopies separately from originals in case anything needs to be handed over.
- If any documents are in a language other than English, check whether a certified translation is required for your specific document type and consulate.
- Avoid bringing loose, unsorted stacks of paper — a few minutes of organizing beforehand saves time and stress at the window.

## Common document mistakes worth avoiding

- **Bringing documents you can't explain.** If you don't understand what a document says, either learn it before your interview or leave it out unless it's specifically required.
- **Outdated financial documents.** Bank statements or sponsor letters that are many months old can look disconnected from your current situation — check what timeframe is actually expected.
- **Missing signatures.** Forms like the I-20 that require a signature from you and an official are sometimes brought incomplete.
- **Assuming last year's list still applies.** Requirements can change — re-check the current list for your visa type and consulate rather than relying on what a friend needed for a different application.

## A simple way to review before interview day

1. Gather everything specified in your appointment confirmation and official checklist for your visa type.
2. Read through each document rather than just packing it — you should be able to explain what it says.
3. Cross-check dates and details against your DS-160.
4. Organize documents in a folder in a logical order, rather than a loose stack.
5. Practice explaining the story behind your documents — not reciting them — in a practice interview.
