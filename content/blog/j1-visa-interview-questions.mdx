J1 covers a wide range of exchange programs — university students, visiting scholars, interns, trainees, au pairs, and work-and-travel participants. The interview questions vary somewhat by category, but they generally circle back to the same core ideas: what is your program, who is sponsoring it, and do you intend to return home afterward.

## What a J1 interview is actually checking

Officers are trying to confirm that your exchange program is genuine, that you understand your role in it, that your funding is credible, and that you have sufficient ties to your home country consistent with the exchange visitor category.

## Common J1 interview topics

### Program purpose and sponsor

Practice questions:

- What is your program, and who is the sponsor organization?
- Why were you selected for this program?
- What will you actually be doing day to day?

Know your program category and your sponsor's role clearly — this is often the first thing an officer will ask about.

### Funding

Practice questions:

- How is your program funded — stipend, personal savings, sponsor support?
- If there's a gap between program costs and your funding, how will you cover it?

### Plans during and after the program

Practice questions:

- What do you plan to do once your program ends?
- Do you intend to return to your home country afterward?
- If you're a student or scholar, how does this exchange connect to your studies or career at home?

### Home country ties

Practice questions:

- What ties do you have to your home country — family, work, or study commitments?
- Why are you doing this exchange instead of a similar opportunity at home?

## Sample J1 interview questions to practice with

1. What is your J1 program category, and what will you be doing?
2. Who is your program sponsor?
3. How is your participation funded?
4. What are your plans once the program ends?
5. What ties do you have to your home country?
6. Have you traveled internationally before, or held a U.S. visa?

## Mistakes that weaken J1 interview answers

- **Vague program descriptions.** Being unable to clearly explain what your program actually involves can raise doubts about its legitimacy, even when it's genuine.
- **Unclear funding.** If your funding source or amount doesn't clearly cover your program costs, address the gap directly.
- **Thin home-country ties.** As with other nonimmigrant categories, weak or vague answers about your plans to return can be a sticking point.
- **Confusing your category's specifics.** An au pair program and a research scholar program involve very different day-to-day realities — be precise about your own.
