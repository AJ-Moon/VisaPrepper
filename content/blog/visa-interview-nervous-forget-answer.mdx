Feeling worried before a visa interview is understandable. You may know your plans but struggle to explain them out loud. Practice gives you a chance to notice that before the real day.

You do not need to sound like a newsreader. Start with short, truthful answers.

## Start with the question you fear most

Write down one question that makes you stop. It might be “Who will pay?” or “Why this course?”

Do not write a polished speech. Write three facts you need to explain. For funding, these might be the person helping, the confirmed support, and what it covers. Use your real records.

If you cannot find a fact, check it. Speaking with more confidence will not fix missing information.

## Try a short practice routine

1. Read the question once.
2. Put your notes aside.
3. Answer out loud in your own words.
4. Notice where you lost your place.
5. Check that detail, then try again.

You can record yourself on your own device if you feel comfortable. Keep the recording private. Listen for whether you answered the question—not whether you have a particular accent.

Try one answer at a time. A long session is not always the most useful way to practise.

## If your mind goes blank

During practice, pause instead of filling the silence with a guess. Start again with the main fact.

For example, the main fact might be your actual reason for travelling. Add only the detail needed to explain it. This is a speaking exercise, not a script an officer expects.

If you did not understand a question, practise asking for it to be repeated. Do not answer a different question just because it feels easier.

## Practise a new question after the first answer

A memorized paragraph can become difficult when someone asks something unexpected. Ask a friend to follow your answer with a simple question.

If you say a parent is paying, they could ask what support is confirmed. If you say you are visiting family, they could ask how long you plan to stay.

Our [follow-up question guide](/blog/visa-interview-follow-up-questions) has more exercises.

## Preparing in a language you understand

You can organize your thoughts in Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, or another familiar language. Then practise explaining those same facts clearly.

This is not a claim about the language available at an embassy. Follow the instructions for your actual appointment.

## Nervousness is not the same as eligibility

A confident answer does not prove that someone qualifies for a visa. The [official visitor visa guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html) explains that the officer decides eligibility under U.S. law.

Practice is also not treatment for a stammer or an anxiety condition. It does not promise to remove either. Give yourself time and do not judge your progress only by how smoothly you speak.

## Leave each practice with one next step

Choose something small: explain the funding more clearly, check a date, or answer the follow-up directly. Use the next session to work on it.
