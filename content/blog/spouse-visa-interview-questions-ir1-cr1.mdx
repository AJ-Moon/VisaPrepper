If your U.S. citizen husband or wife has petitioned for you and your case is heading to a U.S. embassy or consulate, the spouse visa interview questions you face will come from your own file: how you met, your marriage, your spouse's life in the United States and your DS-260. This guide covers the **consular** IR1/CR1 immigrant visa interview abroad, not USCIS adjustment-of-status interviews inside the United States, which follow a different process.

Applying as the H-4 spouse of an H-1B worker? See our [H4 interview questions guide](/blog/h4-visa-interview-questions). K-1 fiancé(e) visas are a separate category not covered here.

Policy points were checked on 21 November 2026, and example questions are illustrative. The State Department's manual tells officers to [avoid a set pattern of questioning](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050407.html) (9 FAM 504.7-4), so no one can hand you "the" question list.

## IR1, CR1 and which interview you attend

| | IR1 | CR1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Spouse of a U.S. citizen | Spouse of a U.S. citizen, conditional status |
| When it applies | Married two years or more on the day you become a permanent resident | Married less than two years on that day |
| After arrival | Permanent residence without conditions | A two-year green card; you and your spouse file Form I-751 in the 90 days before it expires |

Sources: the State Department's [spouse immigrant visa page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/family-immigration/immigrant-visa-for-spouse.html) and [classification table](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050201.html) (9 FAM 502.1), and USCIS on [conditional residence](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/conditional-permanent-residence) and [removing conditions based on marriage](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/conditional-permanent-residence/removing-conditions-on-permanent-residence-based-on-marriage).

Where you live decides the interview. USCIS explains that when a U.S. citizen's spouse is [inside the United States after a lawful admission or parole](https://www.uscis.gov/family/bring-spouse-to-live-in-US), the couple can file Forms I-130 and I-485 together for USCIS adjustment. When the spouse is outside the United States, the approved I-130 is sent for consular processing, and the embassy or consulate interviews you. Spouses of green-card holders, by contrast, must wait until a visa is available.

## What the consular officer explores

The interview is based on your [DS-260 answers and anything arising from your supporting documents](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050407.html); officers often ask a few DS-260 questions and compare your spoken answers with the form (9 FAM 504.7-4). Officers review the approved petition rather than decide it again, but if one comes to believe you are not entitled to the approved status, the [manual](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050402.html) directs them to return it to USCIS through NVC (9 FAM 504.2-8). That is why relationship questions still matter. Officers are also told to interview fairly and give applicants time to answer (9 FAM 504.7-3).

Under 9 FAM 504.7-5, the officer also gives spouse applicants a USCIS pamphlet on the [legal rights of immigrant victims of domestic violence](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/imbra.html) and reviews its main points with you.

## Spouse visa interview questions, by topic

| Topic | Example questions (illustrative) | What a clear answer includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you met | How did you meet? Who introduced you? When did you first meet in person? | Date, place, and who introduced you or which app or event |
| Relationship history | How many times have you met in person? When did you decide to marry? Who proposed? | A rough timeline of visits and the engagement |
| Wedding | Where was the wedding? Who attended? Who paid for it? Did your spouse's family come? | Date and place matching your marriage certificate |
| Communication | How do you keep in touch? Which language do you speak together? When did you last talk? | Your real habits: calls, messages, visits |
| Your spouse's life | What does your husband do? Who is his employer? Which city does your wife live in, and who lives with her? | Job in plain words, employer, city and household |
| Differences | There is a big age gap, or you come from different faiths or countries. How did your families react? | An honest, specific answer, not a defensive one |
| Earlier marriages and children | Was either of you married before? Do either of you have children? Where will they live? | How each earlier marriage ended, matching your documents |
| Living plans | Where will you live? Will you work? | The U.S. address you gave and realistic plans |
| Financial support | Who is your sponsor? Is there a joint sponsor? What does your spouse earn? | Who signed Form I-864, and any joint sponsor |
| Immigration history | Have you visited the U.S. before? Any refusals or overstays? Any other visa applications? | Visas, dates and outcomes matching your DS-260 |

For other visa categories, see our [U.S. visa interview questions guide](/blog/us-visa-interview-questions). Family-specific practice topics are on our [family and immigrant visa page](/visa-types/family-immigrant).

If your families introduced you or you spent most of your engagement apart, say so, and explain how many times you met in person.

## Build each answer with "date, detail, document"

1. **Date.** Anchor the answer in time: "We met in August 2022."
2. **Detail.** Add one concrete fact: "at my cousin's engagement in Lahore."
3. **Document.** Know where that fact appears in your file (I-130, DS-260, marriage certificate), so a follow-up about it does not surprise you.

Then stop. If you are unsure of an exact date, say so and give the closest truthful answer. Our guides to [visa interview follow-up questions](/blog/visa-interview-follow-up-questions) and [what to do if you forget an answer](/blog/visa-interview-nervous-forget-answer) help with the next step.

**Example (illustrative), Pakistan.** Ayesha lives in Karachi; her husband Omar is a U.S. citizen and pharmacist in Chicago.

- Vague: "Our families arranged it, and we got married."
- Clearer: "Our mothers introduced us in March 2023. We talked on video calls for four months, then Omar visited Karachi in July and we agreed to marry. The wedding was in Karachi that December, and our nikah was registered the same week."

**Example (illustrative), India.** Rahul lives in Hyderabad; his wife Sarah is a U.S. citizen.

- Vague: "We met through work and fell in love."
- Clearer: "We met on a project in 2021, when Sarah's company sent her to Bengaluru for a year. She moved back to Austin in 2022 and visited me twice. We married in Hyderabad in May 2024."

These people are invented; use only your own details.

## The consular process, step by step

1. **USCIS approves Form I-130.** Approval [does not by itself give your relative any status](https://www.uscis.gov/i-130), but for an immediate relative, such as a U.S. citizen's spouse, an immigrant visa is always available.
2. **The National Visa Center (NVC) prepares your case.** Under the [Foreign Affairs Manual](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050401.html) (9 FAM 504.1-2), you are "documentarily complete" once you have paid the fees, completed a DS-260, returned Form I-864 with supporting documents and provided the required police certificates. NVC's [document submission step](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-8-scan-collected-documents/step-9-upload-and-submit-scanned-documents.html) also asks you to upload every required civil document before it reviews your case. NVC then schedules the interview for most consular sections, generally in your [country of residence](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/adjudicating-iv-applicants-in-their-country-of-residence.html), or your country of nationality if you ask.
3. **Medical examination.** Every immigrant visa applicant must see a doctor from the embassy's approved [panel of physicians](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/section-42.66) (22 CFR 42.66). Your appointment instructions explain when and where.
4. **The interview.** You must [appear in person](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/section-42.62), and the officer may require you to answer any question material to your classification and eligibility (22 CFR 42.62). Your U.S. citizen spouse does not have to attend, according to the State Department's [applicant interview page](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-10-prepare-for-the-interview/step-11-applicant-interview.html); whether a petitioner may come inside depends on the embassy. The State Department's [interview preparation step](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-10-prepare-for-the-interview.html) and your post's instructions are the final checklist.

As of 21 November 2026, the State Department's [fee schedule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/section-22.1) lists $325 per person for an immediate-relative immigrant visa application and $120 for affidavit of support review when reviewed domestically. The separate [USCIS Immigrant Fee](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-fees/uscis-immigrant-fee) comes after the visa is issued; USCIS strongly encourages paying it online before you travel, and the green card is not issued until it is paid. Confirm current amounts on official pages.

### Check nationality restrictions before you pay

Under [Presidential Proclamation 10998](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/19/2025-23570/restricting-and-limiting-the-entry-of-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states), in effect since 1 January 2026, entry as an immigrant is suspended for nationals of every country it lists for full or partial restrictions who were outside the United States without a valid visa on the effective date. Exceptions include dual nationals traveling on a passport from a country that is not listed. These exceptions replaced those in the June 2025 proclamation, and they no longer include the earlier exception for spouses of U.S. citizens. National-interest exceptions are decided case by case. If your nationality is listed, read the State Department's [guidance on the suspension](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/suspension-of-visa-issuance-to-foreign-nationals-to-protect-the-security-of-the-united-states.html) before paying fees or traveling. Practice cannot change eligibility under these rules.

Separately, the State Department says its January 2026 [immigrant visa pause for nationals of 75 countries](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/immigrant-visa-processing-updates-for-nationalities-at-high-risk-of-public-benefits-usage.html) ended under a court order effective 21 August 2026. Because processing notices changed during 2026, confirm your appointment status directly with NVC and your embassy or consulate.

## Check your DS-260 against the I-130

Your spouse filed the I-130; you filed the DS-260. Compare them before you practice.

| Check | Where it appears | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Names | I-130, DS-260, civil documents | Spellings, maiden names, other names used |
| Marriage date and place | I-130, DS-260, marriage certificate | The same details everywhere |
| Earlier marriages | I-130, DS-260, divorce or death records | Every prior marriage of both spouses, and how it ended |
| Addresses | I-130, DS-260 | Gaps or overlapping dates |
| Children | DS-260 | All children, including from earlier relationships |
| U.S. travel and refusals | DS-260 | Visa types, dates and outcomes |

If you find an error, do not memorize it to stay "consistent". Follow NVC's or your post's correction instructions. At the interview, the regulation says [the required changes shall be made](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/section-42.67) before you sign your DS-260 under oath (22 CFR 42.67), and you affirm it is true, subject to the federal false-statement law (18 U.S.C. 1001), as the [manual](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050401.html) notes.

## Affidavit of support basics (Form I-864)

USCIS says most family-based immigrants use [Form I-864](https://www.uscis.gov/i-864) to show adequate means of financial support. Your U.S. citizen spouse, as petitioner, is the sponsor, and the form is a legally binding contract with the U.S. government. As of 21 November 2026:

- The sponsor includes their most recent federal tax return with W-2s (or explains why they did not file) and may add pay stubs or an employer letter.
- Income generally has to reach 125 percent of the federal poverty guidelines for the household size, as shown in USCIS's [Form I-864P poverty guidelines](https://www.uscis.gov/i-864p) (100 percent for certain active-duty military sponsors of a spouse or child). Household income, assets or a joint sponsor can also count.
- USCIS has accepted only the I-864 edition dated 08/24/26 since 1 October 2026. It includes a privacy release allowing USCIS to request the sponsor's consumer (credit) reports, so a sponsor with a credit freeze should respond quickly to any request to lift it. In an NVC case, follow NVC's instructions.

The manual treats a missing document, such as a tax return, as a documentary 221(g) issue, while insufficient support even after any joint sponsor leads to a [public charge refusal](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM030208.html) (9 FAM 302.8). Officers also weigh the factors the law lists, including your age, health, family status, assets, resources and financial status, and education and skills, so know your own situation as well as the sponsor's. As of 21 November 2026, the State Department also says it is piloting [public charge bonds](https://travel.state.gov/en/newsroom/visas/2026/public-charge-bonds-for-immigrant-visa-applicants.html) for select immigrant visa applications; a consular officer tells you if one is required.

## Civil documents depend on your country

The [regulation on supporting documents](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/section-42.65) (22 CFR 42.65) and the manual's [document requirements](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM050404.html) (9 FAM 504.4-4) ask for these, if obtainable:

- Original or certified copies of your birth certificate and every marriage certificate
- Proof that every earlier marriage of you and your U.S. citizen spouse ended: a final divorce decree, death certificate or annulment
- Police certificates if you are 16 or older: from your current country of residence and your country of nationality if you lived there six months or more, and from any other country where you lived a year or more since turning 16. At any age, if you were ever arrested somewhere, you need a certificate from that country.
- Military records if you ever served in any country's armed forces
- Court and prison records if you were ever convicted
- A valid passport

Acceptable certificates differ by country. Look up yours in the State Department's [reciprocity and civil documents tool](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country.html) and read its [civil documents step](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/the-immigrant-visa-process/step-5-collect-financial-evidence-and-other-supporting-documents/step-7-collect-civil-documents.html), which also requires certified English translations of documents not in English or the official language of the country where you apply, with the translator's signed statement of accuracy and competence. If a record truly cannot be obtained, the officer may accept other satisfactory evidence. If something is missing on the day, the manual says you are generally still interviewed, then refused under 221(g) until you provide it (9 FAM 504.7-2), but some posts reschedule instead: the [U.S. Embassy in the Philippines](https://ph.usembassy.gov/visas/) says immigrant visa applicants who arrive without all required original documents will not be interviewed and must book a new appointment.

### Evidence of your relationship

Many embassies' immigrant visa instructions also ask spouses to bring evidence of an ongoing relationship, and each post sets its own list, as the instructions for [Manila](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Supplements/Supplements_by_Post/MNL-Manila.html) and [Kigali](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Supplements/Supplements_by_Post/KGL-Kigali.html) show. Check your own post's instructions. Typical examples, not requirements everywhere:

- Photos of you together from different times, including the wedding
- Printed call logs, messages or emails across the relationship
- Passport stamps, tickets or boarding passes from visits
- Money transfer receipts or shared finances

Bring originals or real records only, printed if your post does not allow phones. The officer may ask about anything you hand over.

## A practice plan for the weeks before

1. **Gather your file:** the I-130 as filed, your DS-260, the I-864 and your civil documents.
2. **Build the timeline together, then practice separately.** Consistency should come from a real shared history, not a memorized script.
3. **Answer aloud** in short answers, using date, detail, document.
4. **Add follow-ups:** when, where, who else was there?
5. **Fix facts, not phrasing.** If practice exposes a gap, check the document.
